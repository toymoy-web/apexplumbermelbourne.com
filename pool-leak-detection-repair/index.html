Why It Happens

Pool Leak Detection & Repair In Melbourne

Melbourne's sandy, shifting soil affects buried pool plumbing lines the same way it affects other underground pipe on a property. That's a common source of a cracked or separated fitting where a line meets the equipment pad or a pool fitting. Ground movement around a pool's plumbing tends to be gradual, which is why a leak here often develops slowly rather than appearing all at once.

Older pool equipment pads in established Melbourne neighborhoods often have PVC connections that have been through years of sun exposure and ground movement, and a fitting that's become brittle or slightly separated is a frequent, fixable source of water loss. UV exposure specifically makes exposed PVC more brittle over time than the same material would be if fully buried or shaded.

Signs To Watch For

Common Warning Signs

Water Loss Beyond Evaporation

Losing noticeably more water than a bucket test would explain from evaporation alone.

Wet Spots Near The Equipment Pad

Standing water or unusually soggy ground around the pump, filter, or plumbing connections.

Air Bubbles In Return Lines

Air bubbling from a return jet, which can indicate a leak on the suction side pulling in air.

Rising Water Bill With A Pool Auto-Fill

An unexplained water bill increase on a pool with an automatic fill system masking the actual water loss.

Cracking Or Settling Near The Pool Deck

Visible cracking in decking near the pool, sometimes a sign of water escaping and softening the soil beneath it.

How We Diagnose It

Finding The Real Cause

We start with a bucket test to confirm water loss exceeds normal evaporation, then pressure-test the pool's plumbing lines section by section to isolate which line is losing pressure. Dye testing at suspected fitting locations can pinpoint a specific leak point without draining the pool.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if it's a leak or just evaporation?

A bucket test compares your pool's water loss against a control bucket over 24 hours. Losing meaningfully more than the bucket is a real sign of a leak rather than normal evaporation.

Do you repair the pool shell itself?

We handle plumbing-side leaks, lines, fittings, and equipment pad connections. If testing points to the structural shell itself, we'll tell you directly and can point you toward a pool specialist for that specific repair.

Will you need to drain my pool to find the leak?

Not usually. Pressure testing and dye testing can typically isolate a plumbing leak without draining the pool, which saves both time and the cost of refilling it.

Can a leak affect my pool equipment?

Yes, a suction-side leak pulling in air can cause pump priming issues and reduced circulation efficiency over time, which is worth addressing beyond just the water loss itself, since it can shorten the pump's service life if left unresolved.
