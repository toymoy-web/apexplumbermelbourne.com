Water filtration installation addresses taste, odor, and specific contaminants in Melbourne's water supply, which is a distinct concern from the hardness a water softener treats. Melbourne's municipal water blends Floridan Aquifer groundwater with Lake Washington surface water and is chlorinated for disinfection, which some households notice as a taste or odor at the tap even though the water meets safety standards. We test what's actually in your water before recommending a system, rather than defaulting to a generic filter that may not address your actual complaint.

Why It Happens

Water Filtration Installation In Melbourne

City of Melbourne water is treated with chlorine for disinfection as required by state drinking water standards. While this makes the water safe, some households are sensitive to the taste or smell it leaves, particularly right after treatment adjustments. This is a normal, expected byproduct of disinfection rather than a sign of a problem with the water itself, and it can vary noticeably from week to week depending on the utility's current treatment levels.

Melbourne's blended aquifer and surface water supply can also carry sediment and mineral particulates that a whole-home sediment filter catches before they reach fixtures and appliances, which is a separate function from a water softener's job of removing dissolved hardness minerals. A home can genuinely benefit from both systems addressing two different aspects of the same water supply.

Signs To Watch For

Common Warning Signs

Chlorine Taste Or Smell

A noticeable chlorine taste or odor at the tap, common in municipal water and easily addressed with the right filter media.

Cloudy Or Sediment-Heavy Water

Visible cloudiness or particulates, especially right after utility maintenance work in your area.

Relying On Bottled Water For Taste

A household that's switched to bottled water primarily because of tap water taste rather than a safety concern.

Existing Softener Not Addressing Taste Or Odor

A home with a softener that handles hardness but still has taste or odor complaints, since softening and filtration solve different problems.

Fixtures Showing Sediment Buildup

Faucet aerators and showerheads clogging with fine particulate faster than mineral scale alone would explain.

FAQs

Common Questions

What's the difference between a softener and a filtration system?

A softener removes dissolved hardness minerals like calcium and magnesium. Filtration addresses taste, odor, and sediment, which are separate issues. Many Melbourne homes benefit from both, since hard water and chlorine taste are both real, independent factors here, and each system solves a different complaint.

Will a whole-home filter reduce my water pressure?

A correctly sized system shouldn't cause a noticeable pressure drop. We size the filter to your household's actual flow rate and usage before installation, rather than installing a one-size-fits-all unit.

Is Melbourne's tap water actually safe to drink?

The City of Melbourne's water meets state and federal drinking water standards; filtration addresses taste, odor, and sediment preference rather than a safety failure. We don't make specific health claims about water quality beyond what the utility's own published testing shows, and we encourage anyone with specific health concerns to review that testing directly.

How often does a whole-home filter need maintenance?

Carbon filter media typically needs replacement on a schedule based on your household's water usage, which we'll set clearly at installation so you know what to expect going forward, rather than guessing when it's time for a change.
