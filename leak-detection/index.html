Emergency Leak Detection in Melbourne, FL

Leak detection finds a water leak you can't see, most often a slab leak, a leak inside a wall, or a service line leak between the meter and the house. In Melbourne, FL, that usually means tracing a leak through a concrete slab foundation or through a wall shared with a bathroom, since most homes here were built on slab rather than a basement or crawlspace. We use acoustic and thermal equipment to find the leak before any concrete or drywall gets opened, so the repair targets the actual spot instead of a guess.

Not Sure What's Wrong?

Water Bill Went Up And You Don't Know Why?

Most leak detection calls in Melbourne start the same way: a water bill that jumped for no obvious reason, or a water meter that keeps spinning even with every fixture in the house shut off. Homeowners often tell us they don't know where the leak actually is, only that something is using water they can't account for. That's exactly the situation leak detection equipment is built to solve without opening up your floor or walls first.

Why It Happens

Leak Detection In Melbourne

Melbourne's municipal water is drawn from Floridan Aquifer wells and blended with surface water from Lake Washington, and independent testing consistently rates it hard to very hard. That mineral load accelerates pinhole corrosion in older copper supply lines, which is one of the more common sources of a slow, hidden leak in homes built before the 1990s near downtown and the older sections around Eau Gallie and Crane Creek.

Most residential construction across Brevard County sits on a concrete slab foundation, with supply and drain lines running either through the slab or just beneath it. Sandy, limestone-influenced soil shifts through Florida's wet and dry seasons, which stresses those buried lines over time and is a frequent contributor to the slab and service-line leaks we trace across Melbourne.

Signs To Watch For

Common Warning Signs

Water Meter Still Moving

Every fixture is off, but the meter dial keeps turning or the digital reading keeps climbing.

Unexplained Bill Increase

A water bill that jumps well above your normal usage with no new appliance or habit to explain it.

Warm Spot On The Floor

A patch of flooring that's noticeably warmer than the rest, often a sign of a hot water line leaking under the slab.

Low Water Pressure

A steady drop in pressure at fixtures that isn't tied to the city system, which can point to a leak upstream of the fixture.

How We Diagnose It

Finding The Real Cause

Finding a hidden leak starts with isolating which zone of the plumbing system is actually losing water, using pressure testing to narrow down supply versus drain lines before any equipment goes near the slab. From there, acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound signature of water escaping under pressure, and thermal imaging can spot a temperature difference on the slab surface where a hot water line is leaking underneath. For leaks inside a wall or under a fixture, a video inspection camera can confirm the exact point of failure before we cut anything open.

Repair Options

What Fixing It Actually Involves

Spot Repair

If the leak is isolated to one section of pipe, we can often cut in and repair just that section without a full slab excavation.

Reroute Above Slab

For a recurring problem in an aging copper line, rerouting the line through the attic or a wall chase avoids disturbing the slab entirely.

Documentation For Insurance

We provide a written diagnosis of the leak's cause and location, which most homeowner's policies require for a water-damage claim, and repairs are performed to Florida Building Code plumbing provisions.

FAQs

Common Questions

Can you find a leak without tearing up my floor?

In most cases, yes. Acoustic and thermal equipment locates the leak first, so any opening we make targets that specific spot instead of exploratory demolition across the slab.

How do I know if this is a slab leak or something else?

A warm patch on the flooring, a rising water bill, and a moving meter with everything off are the three most common signs. We confirm with pressure testing and acoustic equipment before quoting any repair.

Will my homeowner's insurance cover the cost?

Many policies cover the resulting water damage, though not always the plumbing repair itself. We provide the written diagnosis your insurer will typically ask for, and can point you to what to document before repair begins.

Do you serve properties outside the main city near Eau Gallie or West Melbourne?

Yes, our leak detection dispatch covers Melbourne proper along with West Melbourne, Palm Bay, Indialantic, Satellite Beach, and Melbourne Beach.
