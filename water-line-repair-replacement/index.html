Emergency Water Line Repair & Replacement in Melbourne, FL

Water line repair and replacement fixes the main service line running from your water meter or curb stop to your house, a distinct line from the plumbing inside the home. In Melbourne, this line often runs through the same sandy, shifting soil that stresses other buried pipe, and a leak here shows up as a meter that keeps spinning with everything in the house shut off, since the meter measures water even when it's leaking before it ever reaches your fixtures.

Not Sure What's Wrong?

Meter Running Even With Everything Off Inside?

Homeowners often describe this exact situation: every fixture inside the house is off, but the water meter keeps moving, or they suspect the leak is somewhere between the meter and the house rather than inside it. That's the clearest sign the problem is on the service line itself, not the home's interior plumbing, and it changes both how we find it and how we fix it.

Why It Happens

Water Line Repair & Replacement In Melbourne

The service line running from the meter to the house sits in the same sandy, limestone-influenced soil that shifts through Brevard County's wet and dry seasons. This stresses this buried line over years just as it does supply lines inside the home.

Older copper service lines are also susceptible to the same pinhole corrosion Melbourne's hard aquifer water causes elsewhere, and because this line runs entirely underground between the meter and the house, a leak here can go unnoticed longer than one inside a wall.

Signs To Watch For

Common Warning Signs

Meter Moving With Everything Off

The clearest sign the leak is on the service line rather than inside the home.

Wet Patch Between Meter And House

Standing water or unusually green, lush grass along the line's path between the meter and the house.

Low Pressure At Every Fixture

A sitewide pressure drop, rather than one affected fixture, suggests the restriction is upstream on the service line.

Rising Water Bill With No Inside Leak Found

A bill increase after ruling out leaks inside the home points to the service line itself.

How We Diagnose It

Finding The Real Cause

We start by isolating whether the leak is before or after the meter using pressure testing, which confirms it's a service-line issue rather than interior plumbing. From there, acoustic equipment traces the leak's location along the line's buried path so we know where to dig before any excavation starts.

Equipment We Use

  • Meter isolation and pressure testing.
  • Acoustic leak tracing along the buried line.
  • Line locator for mapping the service line's path.
Repair Options

Spot Repair

FAQs

Common Questions

How is this different from a regular leak detection call?

Leak detection typically covers plumbing inside the home. This service specifically addresses the buried line between your meter and the house, which uses different isolation and tracing steps since it's a different section of your water supply.

Who's responsible for a leak on the service line, me or the utility?

In most cases, the property owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house; the utility typically maintains the line up to and including the meter itself. We can help clarify where the leak falls once it's located.

Will you need to dig up my whole yard?

Not usually. Acoustic tracing pinpoints the leak's location along the line before any excavation, and trenchless replacement can address a full line with far less digging where your property's layout allows it.

Can a service line leak affect my water pressure everywhere in the house?

Yes. Because this line feeds the entire home, a significant leak or restriction on it can cause a sitewide pressure drop rather than affecting just one fixture.
