Emergency Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Melbourne, FL

Slab leak repair fixes a water line leaking beneath your home's concrete slab foundation, which describes most residential construction across Melbourne and Brevard County. It's a distinct diagnostic and repair problem from a typical hidden leak, since the line runs under several inches of concrete rather than inside an accessible wall. We locate it precisely before any concrete comes up, so the repair opens the smallest possible section of floor.

Not Sure What's Wrong?

Warm Spot On The Floor Or Water Near The Slab Edge?

Homeowners usually describe a slab leak the same way before they know that's what it is. A warm patch on the flooring near a garage or bathroom wall, water showing up under floorboards with no clear source, or dampness right at the edge where the slab meets the foundation. That combination of a warm spot and unexplained moisture is one of the clearest signs of a hot water line leaking under the slab.

Why It Happens

Slab Leak Detection & Repair In Melbourne

Nearly all residential construction across Melbourne and Brevard County sits on a concrete slab foundation, with both hot and cold water lines routed either through or just beneath it. Sandy, limestone-influenced soil shifts through Florida's wet and dry seasons, which stresses those buried lines over years.

Melbourne's hard water, drawn from the Floridan Aquifer, accelerates pinhole corrosion in older copper lines specifically. A slab-routed copper line is far harder to inspect or maintain than an exposed one, so a small leak can run for months before it surfaces as a warm floor patch.

Signs To Watch For

Common Warning Signs

Warm Spot On Flooring

A patch of floor, often tile or laminate, that's noticeably warmer than the surrounding area.

Water Under The Slab Edge

Moisture or standing water at the point where the slab meets the exterior foundation.

Rising Water Bill

A meter that keeps moving with everything off, alongside a bill that jumped for no clear reason.

Low Water Pressure At One Fixture

A single fixture losing pressure while others stay normal, suggesting the leak is on that specific line.

How We Diagnose It

Finding The Real Cause

We isolate the plumbing zones with pressure testing first to confirm the leak is under the slab rather than elsewhere in the system. From there, acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping through the concrete, and thermal imaging can show a temperature difference on the slab surface where a hot line is leaking underneath, which together pinpoint the repair location within a small, targeted area.

Equipment We Use

  • Acoustic leak detection listening equipment.
  • Thermal imaging camera.
  • Zone pressure testing gauges.
Repair Options

What Fixing It Actually Involves

Spot Repair Through The Slab

A small, precisely located section of concrete is opened to repair just the leaking section of pipe.

Reroute Above Slab

The damaged line is abandoned in place and rerouted through the attic or a wall chase, avoiding the slab entirely.

Documentation For Insurance

We provide a written diagnosis of the leak's location and likely cause, which most homeowner's policies require for a slab-leak claim, with repairs completed to Florida Building Code plumbing provisions.

Comparison

Spot Repair vs. Rerouting The Line

Spot Repair Makes Sense When

  • The leak is a single, isolated failure point.
  • The rest of the line has no history of prior leaks.
  • The leak is in an easily accessible section of slab.
  • A smaller, more contained repair is the priority.

Rerouting Makes Sense When

  • The line has a history of repeated leaks.
  • The pipe material itself is aging copper prone to more failures.
  • Avoiding another slab opening in the future is a priority.
  • The line's location under the slab is hard to access directly.

FAQs

Common Questions

Will you have to tear up my whole floor?

In most cases, no. Acoustic and thermal equipment narrows the leak to a small, specific area before we open any concrete, so the repair targets just that section.

How is a slab leak different from a regular hidden leak?

The line runs under several inches of concrete rather than inside an accessible wall, which changes both how we detect it and how the repair is done, a spot repair through the slab or a reroute above it rather than opening drywall.

Is rerouting always better than repairing through the slab?

Not always. A single isolated leak in an otherwise sound line is often cheaper to repair in place; a line with a history of repeated failures is usually better rerouted so it stops being a recurring problem.

Does Melbourne's soil make slab leaks more common?

Sandy, limestone-influenced soil shifts through wet and dry cycles, which stresses buried supply lines over time and is a real contributor to the slab leaks we see across Melbourne and Brevard County.
