How Often Should You Flush a Tankless Water Heater in Florida?

Most manufacturer maintenance schedules assume average water hardness. Melbourne's water is well above average, which changes the real answer.

The Manufacturer Standard

What The Owner's Manual Usually Says

Most tankless water heater manufacturers recommend an annual descaling flush as a baseline maintenance interval. That recommendation is built around a national average water hardness assumption, which is meaningfully softer than what actually comes out of a Melbourne tap. Following the manual's schedule to the letter in this specific market means the unit is likely already accumulating more scale than the manufacturer's interval was designed to handle.

Why Melbourne Is Different

The Real Hardness Numbers Here

Melbourne's blended Floridan Aquifer and Lake Washington supply consistently tests hard to very hard, well above the water hardness level most national maintenance schedules assume. Inside a tankless unit, that mineral content scales up the heat exchanger, the component responsible for actually transferring heat to the water passing through, faster than the manufacturer's generic schedule accounts for.

A More Realistic Interval

What We Actually Recommend Locally

For a Melbourne home without a whole-home water softener, a shorter interval makes more sense than the standard annual recommendation. A descaling flush every 6 to 9 months is more realistic here, particularly for a household with average to above-average hot water usage. A home with an already-installed softener can often stay closer to the manufacturer's original annual schedule, since the softener is removing much of the mineral load before it ever reaches the unit.

How To Tell If You're Overdue

Signs The Interval Has Already Passed

Reduced hot water flow is one sign, along with a noticeably longer wait before hot water reaches the fixture, or the unit displaying an error code. All of these mean scale buildup has already progressed past where a routine flush alone might have prevented it. Catching these signs early and scheduling a flush promptly is still far simpler and cheaper than a repair after the unit throws a hard failure.

What The Flush Actually Involves

The Process Itself

A proper descaling flush circulates a mild descaling solution through the heat exchanger using a pump, dedicated equipment different from anything used on a tank-style water heater. This dissolves accumulated scale without damaging the unit's internal components, restoring flow and efficiency without needing to replace any parts on a unit that's otherwise in good working order.

Why Manufacturers Set A Generic Schedule

The National Average Problem

Manufacturer maintenance schedules are written for a national market. They reflect an average water hardness level across the entire country, including many regions with genuinely soft water where scale accumulates slowly if at all. Melbourne's water, drawn from Floridan Aquifer wells and blended with Lake Washington surface water, sits well above that national average on standard hardness scales. Following a generic schedule here means the manufacturer's assumption simply doesn't match local reality, not because the manual is wrong, but because it wasn't written with this specific water supply in mind.

The Cost Of Skipping It

What Happens If You Don't Flush On Time

Skipping a flush doesn't cause immediate failure, the effects are gradual. Reduced efficiency means the unit works harder to deliver the same water temperature, which shows up as a slowly increasing energy bill before any obvious symptom appears. Left long enough, scale buildup can damage the heat exchanger itself, turning what would have been a routine, inexpensive flush into a genuine component repair or even a full unit replacement. Staying ahead of the interval is meaningfully cheaper than catching up after symptoms appear.

Setting A Reminder That Works

Making This Easy To Actually Follow Through On

The most common reason a tankless unit misses its flush interval isn't disagreement about the schedule, it's simply forgetting. Setting a recurring calendar reminder at installation, tied to the unit's specific install date rather than a vague seasonal guess, removes the guesswork. Some homeowners pair it with another twice-yearly task, like changing HVAC filters, so it becomes part of an existing routine rather than a separate thing to remember on its own.

Does Brand Matter

Do All Tankless Units Scale At The Same Rate

Different manufacturers design their heat exchangers with somewhat different tolerances for mineral buildup. Some higher-end units include built-in scale-monitoring sensors that alert you when a flush is genuinely due rather than relying on a fixed calendar interval. That said, no tankless unit is immune to Melbourne's water, brand differences affect how gracefully a unit handles scale buildup and how clearly it signals when service is needed, not whether scaling happens at all. If your unit doesn't have a built-in scale indicator, the 6-to-9-month interval described above remains the more reliable guide.

DIY Flush Kits

Is This Something You Can Do Yourself

DIY descaling kits exist and can work for a homeowner comfortable with basic plumbing tasks, they typically include a small pump, a bucket, and a descaling solution, connected to the unit's service valves. The process itself isn't complicated, but skipping a step, using the wrong solution concentration, or not fully flushing the solution afterward, can cause more harm than the scale buildup itself. For a first flush or if you're not confident identifying your unit's service valves correctly, having a technician handle it once and walk you through the process is a reasonable way to learn before attempting it solo in the future. Either way, the goal is the same: keeping scale from accumulating to the point it affects performance or damages the unit, regardless of who actually performs the work.

Whichever route you choose, keeping a record of when each flush happened, whether DIY or professional, is worth doing regardless. It's the only reliable way to know if you're actually keeping pace with the shorter interval this market genuinely calls for, rather than guessing based on memory alone. A simple log kept near the unit or in a phone note is usually all it takes.
