What Hurricane Season Does to Brevard County Plumbing

From June through November, Melbourne and Brevard County see a real, measurable pattern in the plumbing calls we take, and it's not random.

The Real Pattern

Sewer Backups Spike, And Here's Why

Intense rainfall during Atlantic hurricane season overwhelms drainage systems in low-lying sections near the Indian River Lagoon, which pushes Melbourne's sewer system toward capacity. That backpressure travels into home laterals, especially ones already carrying some root intrusion or age-related damage, which is exactly why sewer backup and drain clearing calls both climb noticeably from June through November compared to the rest of the year.

This isn't unique to any one neighborhood. Older sections near downtown and Eau Gallie with clay or cast-iron laterals feel it first, but even newer PVC-lateral construction closer to the Palm Bay line can back up when the municipal system itself is under seasonal strain.

The Second Factor

Saturated Soil Stresses Buried Lines

Brevard County's sandy, limestone-influenced soil already shifts through normal wet and dry cycles, and hurricane-season rainfall accelerates that movement. Buried supply and sewer lines, whether under a slab foundation or running from the meter to the house, take real stress from that ground movement. That's a genuine contributor to the uptick in slab leaks and service-line failures we see during and right after major storm events.

What Actually Helps

Real, Practical Preparation Steps

Knowing where your main water shutoff valve is, and confirming it actually turns, before hurricane season is the single most useful thing a homeowner can do. A valve that's seized from years of not being touched won't help during an actual emergency.

If your home has a history of sewer backups, a pre-season camera inspection can catch root intrusion or a developing crack before storm-driven backpressure turns it into an active backup. This is also a reasonable time to confirm your sump pump, if you have one, is actually functional rather than assuming it is.

During an active storm, avoid running large amounts of water, laundry, dishwasher, long showers, if you're already seeing slow drains, since that adds load to a system that may already be near capacity.

After The Storm

What To Check Once It Passes

After a significant storm, a slow drain or gurgling sound that wasn't there before is worth addressing before it becomes a full backup. The same goes for a sudden change in water pressure or a wet spot in the yard that appeared during or right after heavy rain, both can indicate a service line or sewer lateral that took real stress during the event.

Older Vs. Newer Construction

Does Hurricane Season Affect Every Home Equally?

Not quite. Homes with original clay or cast-iron sewer laterals, common in older sections near downtown Melbourne and Eau Gallie, are more vulnerable to storm-driven backup than newer PVC-lateral construction, since aging joints already compromised by root intrusion give storm backpressure an easier path in. That doesn't mean newer construction is immune, a municipal system under seasonal strain can still push back into a perfectly sound lateral, but the risk is measurably higher on older infrastructure.

Barrier-island properties in Indialantic, Satellite Beach, and Melbourne Beach face an additional factor: direct storm surge exposure alongside the same rainfall-driven backup risk the mainland sees. That's on top of the ongoing salt-air corrosion these towns deal with year-round, which is a separate, slower-moving issue from acute storm damage.

A Realistic Timeline

When To Start Preparing

Atlantic hurricane season officially runs June through November, but the practical window for preparation is really late spring, before the first named storm puts pressure on everyone's schedule at once. A pre-season camera inspection, valve check, and sump pump test all take relatively little time individually. Doing them ahead of the season rather than during an active storm watch means you're not competing with every other homeowner in Brevard County for the same appointment slots.

The Water Damage Distinction

Plumbing Repair Versus Water Damage Restoration

It's worth understanding the difference between a plumbing repair and water damage restoration, since hurricane season brings both, and they're genuinely different services. A burst pipe or backed-up sewer line is a plumbing repair, fixing the component that failed. The resulting flooding, drying out affected areas, and preventing mold growth is water damage restoration, a distinct service category with its own equipment and expertise. A single storm event can require both, and knowing which is which helps you get the right help faster instead of calling the wrong specialist first.

This distinction also matters for insurance purposes. Documenting which part of the damage was the plumbing failure itself versus the resulting water damage can matter for how a claim gets processed, so it's worth asking whoever responds to clarify which category their work falls under.

Building A Simple Checklist

What To Do Before Hurricane Season Arrives

None of these steps take more than an hour combined. All of them are far easier to handle in May than during an active hurricane watch in September, when every plumber in Brevard County is fielding the same calls at once and appointment windows fill up fast.

Locate The Main Shutoff

Confirm its exact location and that it turns freely, not just that you know roughly where it is.

Save A Plumber's Number Offline

Keep it somewhere you'll actually find it during a power outage, not only in a phone that might be dead.

Get A Pre-Season Camera Inspection

If your home has a history of drain or sewer issues, do this before storm season rather than during it.

Test Your Sump Pump

Don't assume it works because it did last year, run it before the season starts.

The Bottom Line

Predictable Stress, Not Random Bad Luck

The pattern this article describes, sewer backups climbing with rainfall, buried lines stressed by saturated soil, older infrastructure feeling it first, is genuinely predictable rather than random. That predictability is exactly why preparation ahead of the season pays off more here than in a market without this seasonal pressure, and why we treat hurricane-season calls as an expected part of the year rather than a surprise.
