What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives for a Burst Pipe

The minutes before help arrives matter more than most homeowners realize. Here's what actually limits damage during that window.

Step One

Shut Off The Main Water Valve

This is the single most important action, and it should happen before anything else, before calling anyone, before trying to contain the water. Every minute the valve stays open, more water floods the affected area. If you've located and tested your main shutoff valve ahead of time, this takes seconds. If you haven't, this is exactly the situation that makes knowing its location in advance so valuable.

Step Two

Shut Off Electricity To The Affected Area If Safe

Water and electricity are a dangerous combination. If the burst pipe is anywhere near outlets, switches, or electrical fixtures, and you can safely reach your breaker panel without walking through standing water, shut off power to that area. If reaching the panel would require walking through water, don't risk it, wait for a professional instead.

Step Three

Move Valuables And Start Documenting

Once the water is shut off, move furniture, electronics, and valuables away from the affected area if it's safe to do so. Take photos or video of the damage before any cleanup begins, this documentation matters for insurance purposes and is much harder to recreate after the fact.

Step Four

Call For Help And Describe What You're Seeing

When you call, be ready to describe where the water is coming from, how much is flowing, and whether it's still increasing. This helps dispatch prioritize genuinely urgent calls and helps the technician arrive better prepared for what they'll find. Our emergency line is answered 24/7, and the dispatcher can also walk you through your main shutoff's likely location if you're still searching for it.

What Not To Do

Mistakes That Make Things Worse

Don't attempt to repair the pipe yourself with tape or a clamp as a permanent fix, temporary patches on a pressurized line often fail suddenly and can make the eventual repair more complicated. Don't run additional water anywhere in the house while waiting for help, that adds more volume to an already flooding situation. And don't wait to see if it gets better on its own, a burst pipe doesn't self-resolve, and the damage compounds with every additional minute of flow.

After The Water Stops

What Happens Once We Arrive

Once on-site, we confirm the exact break point using the same acoustic and camera equipment we'd use for any hidden leak, then repair or replace the affected section. Repairs meet the standards incorporated into the Florida Building Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). If flooding has caused water damage beyond the pipe itself, drying and mold prevention is a separate service category, water damage restoration, from the plumbing repair, and we'll tell you clearly which one applies to what you're looking at.

Insurance Considerations

What To Keep In Mind For A Claim

The photos and video you take before cleanup begins matter more than most homeowners realize during an active emergency, but they're genuinely important for an insurance claim afterward. Most policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, though coverage details vary, and having clear documentation of the damage's extent before any repair work starts gives your adjuster the clearest picture of what actually happened. A written diagnosis from the plumber who repairs the pipe, showing the cause and location of the break, is also worth requesting for your records.

Preventing The Next One

What This Event Might Be Telling You

A single burst pipe can be an isolated event, a specific weak point that finally gave way. But if it's not the first pipe failure in your home, or if the burst section was part of an aging copper or galvanized system, it's worth asking whether this is a warning sign about the broader plumbing system rather than a one-off. A technician can give you an honest read on whether continued spot repairs make sense or whether the underlying pipe material is genuinely reaching the end of its service life.

For Renters And Property Managers

A Different Set Of First Steps

If you're renting, the shutoff and documentation steps above still apply, water doesn't wait for a lease agreement to sort out responsibility. Shut off the main valve if you can, document the damage, and contact your property manager or landlord immediately alongside calling for emergency plumbing help. Most rental agreements require prompt notification of property damage, and acting quickly protects both your belongings and your position if a dispute arises later about how the damage was handled.

What If You're Not Home

Planning For A Burst Pipe While You're Away

A burst pipe while you're traveling is a genuinely harder situation, since the delay before anyone notices the flow can turn a contained repair into significant water damage. If you travel frequently, consider a smart water shutoff device, which monitors flow and can automatically close your main valve if it detects a leak pattern consistent with a burst pipe, even with no one home to react. Short of that investment, arranging for a neighbor or family member to check the house periodically during extended absences at least shortens the window between failure and discovery.

The Bigger Picture

Why Preparation Beats Reaction Every Time

Everything in this article assumes you're reacting to an emergency already in progress. The genuinely better position is knowing your shutoff valve's location and confirming it works before you ever need these steps, since the fastest possible reaction still loses precious minutes if you spend them searching for a valve you've never actually located. Preparation doesn't prevent a pipe from bursting, but it meaningfully limits how much damage happens in the minutes before help arrives.

If reading this has made you realize you don't actually know where your own shutoff valve is, that's worth addressing today, not after the next emergency makes it urgent.
