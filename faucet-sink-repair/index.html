Faucet & Sink Repair in Melbourne, FL

Faucet and sink repair covers a dripping faucet, a valve that won't fully shut off, a leaking supply line under the sink, or replacing a fixture entirely. In Melbourne, hard water from the Floridan Aquifer is the leading cause of a faucet cartridge or valve wearing out faster than it should, since mineral deposits build up on the internal seals every time water passes through. We diagnose which specific part has failed before recommending a repair or a full fixture replacement.

Why It Happens

Faucet & Sink Repair In Melbourne

Mineral scale from Melbourne's hard water accumulates on a faucet's internal cartridge or valve seals, which is the most common reason a faucet starts dripping or a shutoff valve gets increasingly difficult to turn. This buildup happens gradually, so most homeowners notice the drip long before they'd think to check the actual cartridge.

Homes near downtown and Eau Gallie built before the 1990s sometimes still have original supply line valves under the sink, which corrode with age and are more likely to seize or leak than the quarter-turn valves used in newer construction. A seized valve is often discovered only when it's needed in an emergency, which is why we check and replace them proactively during any sink repair visit.

Signs To Watch For

Common Warning Signs

Persistent Dripping

A faucet that continues dripping after being fully shut off, usually a worn cartridge or washer.

Valve Won't Shut Off Fully

A shutoff valve under the sink that no longer fully stops water flow when closed.

Leak At The Base

Water pooling at the base of the faucet, often a failed O-ring rather than the cartridge itself.

Low Flow At One Fixture

Reduced water flow from a single faucet while others in the house are normal, often mineral buildup inside the aerator or valve.

Stiff Or Squeaky Handle

A handle that's become hard to turn or squeaks during use, a sign of internal corrosion or dried-out lubrication.

How We Diagnose It

Finding The Real Cause

Most faucet repairs are diagnosed by disassembling the handle to inspect the cartridge, valve seat, and O-rings directly. For a leak under the sink, we check the supply line connections and shutoff valve first, since a loose compression fitting is a more common cause than the faucet itself.

Equipment We Use

  • Cartridge and valve seat inspection.
  • Supply line and shutoff valve pressure check.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping after I've replaced the washer myself?

A drip that returns quickly after a DIY fix often means the valve seat itself is worn, not just the washer, which needs professional resurfacing or a cartridge replacement rather than a washer swap alone. We check the seat directly rather than assuming a second washer will hold.

Is a dripping faucet actually worth fixing quickly?

Yes. A slow drip wastes real water over a year and often accelerates once the internal seal wears further, so a small drip now is usually cheaper to fix than the same issue after it worsens. Left long enough, a persistent drip can also stain the sink basin or fixture finish.

Can you match a new faucet to my existing sink?

Yes, we check your sink's existing hole configuration and spacing before recommending a replacement faucet so it fits without modification. If your sink has fewer holes than most new faucets need, we'll walk you through the compatible options rather than assuming one will fit.

Do you repair outdoor and utility sink faucets too?

Yes, the same diagnosis and repair process applies to kitchen, bathroom, laundry, and utility sink faucets, as well as outdoor hose bibs where the failure is at the faucet valve itself.
