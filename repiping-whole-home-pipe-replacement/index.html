Whole-Home Repiping in Melbourne, FL

Repiping replaces the water supply lines running throughout the inside of your home, not the service line from the meter, which is a separate job. It's the right call when a home is experiencing leaks at multiple points rather than one isolated failure, since aging galvanized or corroded copper pipe throughout a house tends to fail in more than one place around the same time.

Not Sure What's Wrong?

Leaks Keep Happening In Different Spots, Not Just One?

A pattern we see often in Melbourne's older housing stock: a leak gets repaired, then another one shows up somewhere else in the house a few months later, unrelated to the first. That's usually a sign the pipe material itself is failing throughout the home from age and corrosion, not a coincidence of bad luck. At that point, repiping the whole system is typically more cost-effective than repeatedly repairing one failure at a time.

Why It Happens

Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement In Melbourne

Homes near downtown Melbourne and Eau Gallie built before the 1970s sometimes still run original galvanized steel supply pipe, which corrodes from the inside over decades. It eventually restricts flow and develops leaks at multiple points as the corrosion progresses unevenly.

Melbourne's hard aquifer water accelerates pinhole corrosion in copper supply lines too, which is why even copper-piped homes from the 1970s-1990s can reach a point where multiple sections are failing around the same time rather than one isolated pipe.

Signs To Watch For

Common Warning Signs

Leaks At Multiple Points

More than one supply-line leak occurring in different parts of the home within a relatively short span.

Discolored Water Sitewide

Rusty or discolored water from multiple fixtures, not just one, pointing to pipe material rather than a single fixture issue.

Low Pressure Throughout The House

A gradual, sitewide pressure decline as corrosion narrows the interior diameter of aging pipe.

Visible Corrosion On Exposed Pipe

Green or white corrosion on visible copper, or rust on visible galvanized pipe, in a basement, attic, or utility area.

How We Diagnose It

Finding The Real Cause

We inspect any accessible sections of your home's supply piping directly, check water pressure and flow at multiple fixtures, and review your home's age and known pipe material history. If a pattern of repeated, unrelated leaks is already established, that history itself is strong evidence for repiping over continuing to chase individual repairs.

Repair Options

What Fixing It Actually Involves

PEX Repiping

Flexible PEX tubing is run through walls and attic spaces with minimal wall-opening compared to rigid pipe, and resists the pinhole corrosion that affects copper.

Copper Repiping

For homeowners who prefer to stay with copper, we run new Type L copper sized correctly for current code.

Phased Repiping

For an occupied home, repiping can be phased room by room or floor by floor to keep water available throughout the project.

FAQs

Common Questions

How is repiping different from fixing the water line to my house?

Repiping replaces the supply lines inside your home. The line from the meter or curb stop to the house is a separate, distinct job, since it's a different section of your water supply with different access and repair methods.

Do I have to move out during repiping?

No, most repiping is done with the home occupied, and water is typically restored at the end of each work day. Phased repiping can also limit disruption to one area at a time.

Is PEX or copper the better choice for Melbourne?

PEX resists the pinhole corrosion that Melbourne's hard water causes in copper, and it's generally faster to install with less wall-opening. Copper remains a solid choice for homeowners who prefer it, and we're glad to install either.

How do I know if I need repiping or just another repair?

A pattern of leaks in different, unrelated locations, sitewide low pressure, or visible corrosion on multiple exposed pipe sections are the clearest signs the pipe material itself is the problem, not a single isolated failure.
